\section{Reviewer-Robustness Experiments}
\label{app:reviewer-robustness}

All studies in this Online Resource section are post-hoc robustness or sensitivity analyses.
Their task selections, seeds, factors, and output paths were frozen before
formal execution, and no result changes the original 56-task discovery set,
84-task held-out set, $\alpha=0.10$ selection, or preregistered H1/H2 family.
An audit confirmed that the original Phase-2 inference already aggregates
tasks within base graph, bootstraps whole graph units, applies graph-level
sign flips, and uses the frozen Holm correction. It therefore requires no
pseudo-replication correction. New intervals below likewise aggregate paired
task effects within graph and resample graphs with equal weight.

\subsection{Optimizer Robustness}

The formal matrix contains 2,016 runs: 56 tasks, three seeds, two depths, two
objectives, and three local optimizers. Each run has a strict cap of 120
actual objective calls. Wrapper and SciPy counts agree on every one of the
1,512 runs for which SciPy returns an \texttt{nfev}; the 504 SLSQP runs stop
at the wrapper cap while numerical finite differences are being evaluated, so
the absent SciPy terminal count is retained as missing rather than imputed.
Termination status, message, iteration count, wall time, and both counters are
stored per run.

Nested failures occur in both O0 and O3 under every optimizer
(\cref{tab:reviewer-optimizer}). The dual-PASS restriction then retains only
task--seed pairs for which neither arm has a positive signed nested regret
above $10^{-10}$. The positive graph intervals and frequent O0
lower-energy/lower-feasibility pairs persist in that restricted population.
The post-hoc COBYLA/O0 count of 28/168 is a separately frozen strict-cap
rerun, not a redefinition of the historical 29/168 observation, which remains
unchanged.
This supports empirical separation of optimizer inadequacy and objective
misalignment under the diagnostic, not a universal statement about either
optimizer or loss landscape.

\subsection{CVaR Tail-Fraction Sensitivity}

The discovery scan completes 1,008/1,008 planned runs with no failed records.
The response peaks at the historically frozen $\alpha=0.10$ in the
equal-weight graph mean, but the nearby 0.05 and 0.25 responses differ by only
$-0.0386$ and $-0.0329$ decades; both graph-cluster intervals for these
differences include zero. By contrast, the optimized response is not assumed
to interpolate monotonically, and 54/56 task trajectories are non-monotone.
Additional held-out scans at $\alpha=0.05,0.25,0.50,1.00$ are labelled
\texttt{POST\_HOC\_SENSITIVITY}; none is used to select a new primary tail
fraction.

The $\alpha=1$ control covers 222 executed discovery and post-hoc held-out
cells and gives a maximum direct objective discrepancy of
$2.42\times10^{-13}$ from mean energy. For the 168 discovery cells matched to
the closest historical O0 control, task, depth, seed, exact embedded-$p=2$
initial parameters, nominal budget, optimizer family, initial trust-region
radius, and constraint tolerance agree. The initial objective discrepancy is
at most $2.02\times10^{-13}$. The historical and post-hoc wrappers and SciPy
versions differ, however, and complete comparable trajectories were not
persisted; their terminal parameters need not coincide. This audit therefore
validates objective equivalence only.

\subsection{Depth--Budget Ablation}

The 24 tasks were selected using graph, size, feasible fraction, and dilution,
without reference to O0/O3 outcomes. The formal run stores one deterministic
480-call trajectory only after a smoke gate confirmed that its first 120 and
240 objective calls match separately capped runs exactly. Each reported
checkpoint is the best evaluated incumbent in that genuine prefix, rather
than necessarily the solver's last evaluated point.

\input{tables/tableS7_depth_budget.tex}

The failure-rate direction and aggregation were independently audited. All 16
structural checks passed, and 24 stratified PASS/FAIL cases were recomputed
from their saved parameters. Maximum absolute discrepancies were
$1.55\times10^{-15}$ for the deeper objective,
$2.22\times10^{-16}$ for the embedded objective, and
$1.42\times10^{-15}$ for signed regret; both embedding identities were exact
at stored precision. The licensed conclusion is limited to the tested subset,
COBYLA, O0/O3, depths, seeds, and budgets: increasing the tested classical
evaluation budget did not monotonically reduce certified nested-ansatz
failures. The positive $p=4-p=3$ feasibility effects in
\cref{tab:reviewer-depth-budget} show why that
diagnostic must not be conflated with final-state quality.

\subsection{Finite-Shot Estimation}

The all-held-out fixed-endpoint protocol and its 20 replicates per cell are
detailed in \cref{app:finite-shot}. A separate outcome-blind 18-task subset
uses 50 sampling seeds to cross
$\alpha\in\{0.02,0.05,0.10,0.25,0.50\}$ with
$N_{\rm shots}\in\{10^3,10^4,10^5\}$. The mean of the separately computed
O0 and O3 CVaR-0.10 RMSE values falls from 0.04839 to 0.01570 and 0.00486 as
shots increase. The heat map in \cref{fig:reviewer-alpha-shots} shows the larger
sampling cost of more aggressive lower tails. No quantum optimization is
rerun for this estimator analysis.

\subsection{Finite-Shot Training}
\label{app:reviewer-finite-shot}

The finite-shot training matrix completes 200/200 runs with no failed records.
Each objective evaluation uses a fresh reproducible sample; final parameters
are evaluated exactly. The two shot-trained point estimates preserve the
exact O3-minus-O0 direction, but both graph-cluster intervals cross zero
(\cref{tab:reviewer-finite-training}). The 10,000-shot condition has positive
effects on eight of ten graphs, compared with six of ten at 1,000 shots and
nine of ten for matched exact training. This is mixed evidence about noisy
training and stronger evidence about fixed-endpoint estimation; it is not a
hardware or device-noise experiment.

\subsection{Classical RCSP Context}

An instrumented exact label-setting solver reproduces the frozen optimum on
140/140 canonical tasks. Across repeated high-resolution solves, the median,
95th-percentile, and maximum per-task medians are 50.0, 95.4, and
188.1 microseconds; generated-label counts have median 13 and maximum 30.
These small descriptive values confirm the intended context: the instances
are classically tractable mechanism probes. They do not define a
quantum--classical runtime comparison.
