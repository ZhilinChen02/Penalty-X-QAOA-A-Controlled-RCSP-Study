\abstract{
\textbf{Purpose} Poor feasible sampling in full-space Penalty-X QAOA can reflect
representation-induced dilution, classical optimizer inadequacy, objective
misalignment, or limited ansatz capacity. We separate these explanations on a
controlled routing benchmark.

\textbf{Methods} Exact-statevector experiments use 140 resource-constrained
shortest-path tasks and a scale-controlled Hamiltonian. Exact depth embeddings
diagnose optimizer inadequacy. A frozen discovery/held-out design compares
mean-energy training (O0), an exact-feasibility capacity control (O2), and
CVaR-0.10 (O3), with post-hoc depth, optimizer, tail-fraction, and finite-shot
checks.

\textbf{Results} O2 showed that the fixed ansatz could reach feasible mass not
induced by O0. On 84 held-out tasks from 15 graphs, O3 improved graph-level
log-feasibility gain over O0 by $+0.3547$ decades and was non-inferior to O2:
the O3--O2 effect was $-0.0086$, with lower bound $-0.0360$ above the frozen
$-0.10$ margin. Certified nested failures occurred under COBYLA,
Nelder--Mead, and SLSQP. Fixed-endpoint sampling was stable, whereas both
finite-shot retraining intervals crossed zero.

\textbf{Conclusion} Optimizer inadequacy and objective misalignment are
distinct under the tested diagnostics, and CVaR approaches the mechanistic O2
capacity control on held-out graphs. The classically tractable study supports
no quantum-advantage or extrapolative scaling claim and does not test hardware
noise.
}
